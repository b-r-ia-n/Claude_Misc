# Facts: Sabbaths (sacred and ritual rest)

Facts gathered for the essay "Forty Less One" (`../01-sabbaths.md`). Entries marked (unused) were left out of the essay.

### God ceases on the seventh day
- **Fact:** Genesis says God finished his work and rested (Hebrew *shavat*, "ceased") on the seventh day, and blessed and sanctified it.
- **Source:** Genesis 2:2-3
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** sabbath, genesis, creation, hebrew-bible

### "And was refreshed"
- **Fact:** Exodus says that on the seventh day God rested *va-yinafash*, a verb related to *nefesh* (breath, soul), often translated "and was refreshed."
- **Source:** Exodus 31:17
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** sabbath, exodus, hebrew, etymology

### No weariness touched God
- **Fact:** The Qur'an, describing the creation of the heavens and earth in six days, adds that no weariness touched God, commonly read as a rejection of the idea that God needed rest.
- **Source:** Qur'an 50:38
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** islam, quran, creation, theology

### Humans made so the gods can rest
- **Fact:** In the Akkadian Atrahasis epic, the lesser gods rebel against the labor of digging canals, and humans are created to bear that labor. Later the noise of multiplying humans keeps Enlil from sleeping, and he sends plague, drought, and finally a flood.
- **Source:** Atrahasis epic (Old Babylonian version); see Lambert and Millard, *Atra-hasis: The Babylonian Story of the Flood* (1969)
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** mesopotamia, myth, gods, labor, sleep

### Shapattu, the full-moon day
- **Fact:** In Babylonian texts the fifteenth day of the month (full moon) was called *shapattu*, glossed as *ūm nūḫ libbi*, "day of the quieting (resting) of the heart." Its relation to the Hebrew Sabbath is debated.
- **Source:** Akkadian lexical texts; discussed in standard Assyriological literature on the Sabbath's origins
- **Status:** disputed
- **Tags:** babylon, calendar, moon, sabbath-origins

### Babylonian evil days
- **Fact:** A Babylonian hemerology from Ashurbanipal's library marks the 7th, 14th, 19th, 21st and 28th days of certain months as evil days, on which the king should not eat meat cooked on coals, change or put on white garments, ride a chariot, or speak as ruler; the seer should not give oracles and the physician should not treat the sick.
- **Source:** Hemerology published in H. Rawlinson, *Cuneiform Inscriptions of Western Asia* IV (pl. 32-33); translated in S. Langdon, *Babylonian Menologies and the Semitic Calendars* (1935)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** babylon, omens, calendar, kingship, taboo

### The nineteenth day as seven sevens
- **Fact:** The 19th day of the month, counted from the first day of the previous (30-day) month, is the 49th day, which is often cited as the reason it was ill-omened.
- **Source:** Langdon (1935) and later commentators; explanation is scholarly interpretation
- **Status:** disputed
- **Tags:** babylon, numerology, seven, calendar

### Manna does not fall on the Sabbath
- **Fact:** Exodus says manna fell six days a week, with a double portion on the sixth day; manna kept overnight bred worms and stank, except the portion kept for the Sabbath.
- **Source:** Exodus 16:19-30
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** exodus, manna, sabbath, wilderness

### No fire in your dwellings
- **Fact:** The Torah forbids kindling fire in one's dwellings on the Sabbath day.
- **Source:** Exodus 35:3
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** sabbath, fire, law, torah

### The stick-gatherer
- **Fact:** Numbers tells of a man found gathering sticks on the Sabbath, who was held in custody because it had not been declared what to do with him, and was then stoned by divine command.
- **Source:** Numbers 15:32-36
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** sabbath, punishment, numbers, law

### Thirty-nine categories of work
- **Fact:** The Mishnah lists the primary categories of labor (*avot melakhot*) forbidden on the Sabbath, counting them as "forty less one," from sowing and plowing to carrying from one domain to another.
- **Source:** Mishnah Shabbat 7:2
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** sabbath, mishnah, labor, law, lists

### Derived from the Tabernacle
- **Fact:** The Talmud links the 39 categories to the labors required to build the Tabernacle, because the Sabbath command is juxtaposed with the Tabernacle instructions in Exodus.
- **Source:** Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 49b
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** talmud, tabernacle, sabbath, labor

### Two letters
- **Fact:** The prohibited labor of writing is defined as writing two letters; writing a single letter is not a full violation.
- **Source:** Mishnah Shabbat 12:3 (and 7:2)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** sabbath, writing, mishnah, thresholds

### The eruv
- **Fact:** An eruv (*eruv chatzerot*) legally merges private properties into a single shared domain so that carrying is permitted within it on the Sabbath; in many modern cities it is a wire or line along utility poles, checked weekly.
- **Source:** Mishnah and Talmud, tractate Eruvin; contemporary community eruv practice
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** eruv, sabbath, boundaries, cities

### Solomon instituted the eruv
- **Fact:** The Talmud attributes the institution of the eruv (and ritual handwashing) to King Solomon, saying a heavenly voice approved.
- **Source:** Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 14b; Eruvin 21b
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** eruv, solomon, talmud, tradition

### Two thousand cubits
- **Fact:** The *techum Shabbat* limits walking on the Sabbath to 2,000 cubits beyond the boundary of one's town.
- **Source:** Mishnah Eruvin 4-5; based on rabbinic reading of Exodus 16:29 and Numbers 35:5
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** sabbath, walking, distance, law

### Akiva's three proofs
- **Fact:** Asked by the Roman governor Turnus Rufus what distinguishes the Sabbath, Rabbi Akiva is said to have cited the Sambation river, which rests on the Sabbath; the necromancer's ghost, which does not rise on it; and Turnus Rufus's father's grave, which does not smoke on it.
- **Source:** Genesis Rabbah 11:5; Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 65b
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** akiva, sambation, rome, midrash, dead

### Pliny's Sabbath stream
- **Fact:** Pliny the Elder reports a stream in Judaea that dries up every Sabbath.
- **Source:** Pliny, *Natural History* 31.18 (31.24 in some numberings)
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** pliny, rivers, sabbath, rome

### Josephus's Sabbatic river
- **Fact:** Josephus describes a "Sabbatic river" in Syria between Arcea and Raphanea that is dry for six days and flows on the seventh, the reverse of Pliny and the rabbis.
- **Source:** Josephus, *Jewish War* 7.5.1
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** josephus, rivers, sabbath, syria

### Seneca on lost sevenths
- **Fact:** Seneca, quoted by Augustine, criticized the Jewish Sabbath, saying the Jews lose almost a seventh of their lives in idleness.
- **Source:** Augustine, *City of God* 6.11, quoting Seneca's lost *De superstitione*
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** seneca, augustine, rome, idleness

### Tacitus on the seventh year
- **Fact:** Tacitus wrote that the Jews gave the seventh day to idleness and, enticed by sloth, the seventh year as well.
- **Source:** Tacitus, *Histories* 5.4
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** tacitus, rome, shmita, idleness

### Juvenal's idle father (unused)
- **Fact:** Juvenal mocks a father for whom every seventh day was a day of idleness, kept from any part of life.
- **Source:** Juvenal, *Satires* 14.105-106
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** juvenal, satire, rome, sabbath

### The sabbatical year
- **Fact:** Every seventh year (*shmita*) the land of Israel is to lie fallow, its produce ownerless, and at its end debts between Israelites are released.
- **Source:** Leviticus 25:1-7; Exodus 23:10-11; Deuteronomy 15:1-2
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** shmita, land, debt, agriculture

### Hillel's prosbul
- **Fact:** Hillel instituted the *prosbul*, a document transferring a loan to the court so it is not cancelled by the sabbatical year, because people had stopped lending as the year approached.
- **Source:** Mishnah Shevi'it 10:3-4; Babylonian Talmud, Gittin 36a
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** hillel, debt, shmita, legal-fiction

### Heter mechira (unused)
- **Fact:** Before the 1888-89 sabbatical year, some rabbis permitted Jewish farmers in Ottoman Palestine to sell their land temporarily to non-Jews so it could be worked; the *heter mechira* is still used and contested in Israel.
- **Source:** Standard histories of the shmita controversy; endorsed by Rabbi Yitzchak Elchanan Spektor, later by Rabbi Abraham Isaac Kook
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** shmita, israel, land-sale, controversy

### The land enjoys its sabbaths
- **Fact:** Leviticus warns that if the people do not keep the sabbatical years they will be exiled and the land will enjoy its sabbaths while desolate; Chronicles presents the seventy years of exile as fulfilling those owed sabbaths (the Chronicles reading is unused in the essay).
- **Source:** Leviticus 26:34-35; 2 Chronicles 36:21
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** shmita, exile, land, babylon

### The Jubilee
- **Fact:** After seven sabbatical cycles, the fiftieth year is a Jubilee, in which land returns to its ancestral holders and Israelite slaves are freed.
- **Source:** Leviticus 25:8-55
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** jubilee, land, liberty, torah

### The Liberty Bell's inscription
- **Fact:** The Liberty Bell bears "Proclaim LIBERTY throughout all the Land unto all the Inhabitants thereof," from the Jubilee passage; the bell is famously cracked.
- **Source:** Leviticus 25:10; inscription on the Liberty Bell, Philadelphia (cast 1752, recast 1753)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** jubilee, liberty-bell, america, inscriptions

### Catholic Holy Years (unused)
- **Fact:** The Catholic Jubilee or Holy Year began with Boniface VIII in 1300 and is now normally held every 25 years; 2025 was a Jubilee year.
- **Source:** Boniface VIII, bull *Antiquorum habet* (1300)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** jubilee, catholic, rome, pilgrimage

### Constantine's Sunday law
- **Fact:** In March 321 Constantine ordered magistrates, city dwellers and workshops to rest on "the venerable day of the Sun," while allowing country people to continue farming, lest the right moment for sowing or planting vines be lost.
- **Source:** Codex Justinianus 3.12.2 (dated 7 March 321)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** constantine, sunday, rome, law

### Peters's blue laws
- **Fact:** The Rev. Samuel Peters's *General History of Connecticut* (1781) listed supposed colonial "blue laws," including that no woman should kiss her child on the Sabbath; historians regard much of the list as fabricated or exaggerated.
- **Source:** Samuel Peters, *A General History of Connecticut* (London, 1781)
- **Status:** disputed
- **Tags:** blue-laws, connecticut, puritans, fabrication

### Blue paper
- **Fact:** A common explanation says blue laws were so called because they were printed on blue paper; there is no evidence for this, and the origin of "blue" is uncertain.
- **Source:** unknown (popular etymology)
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** blue-laws, etymology, folklore

### Bergen County
- **Fact:** Bergen County, New Jersey, which includes the major retail center of Paramus, retains blue laws that close most retail stores on Sundays.
- **Source:** Bergen County ordinances and New Jersey state law permitting local Sunday closing; widely reported
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** blue-laws, new-jersey, shopping, sunday

### McGowan v. Maryland
- **Fact:** In 1961 the U.S. Supreme Court upheld Maryland's Sunday closing laws, holding that whatever their religious origin they now served the secular purpose of a uniform day of rest.
- **Source:** *McGowan v. Maryland*, 366 U.S. 420 (1961)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** sunday, law, supreme-court, secular

### England's Sunday Observance Act (unused)
- **Fact:** England's Sunday Observance Act of 1677 forbade ordinary labor and the sale of goods on the Lord's Day, with exceptions for works of necessity and charity.
- **Source:** Sunday Observance Act 1677 (29 Cha. II c. 7)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** england, sunday, law, puritans

### The Book of Sports (unused)
- **Fact:** James I's Declaration of Sports (1618) affirmed that lawful recreations such as dancing and archery were allowed on Sundays after church, provoking Puritan opposition; Charles I reissued it in 1633.
- **Source:** *The King's Majesty's Declaration to His Subjects Concerning Lawful Sports* (1618; reissued 1633)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** england, sunday, recreation, puritans

### Benedict's seven offices
- **Fact:** The Rule of Saint Benedict prescribes seven daytime offices, citing Psalm 119:164 ("Seven times a day I have praised you"), and a night office, citing Psalm 119:62 ("At midnight I arose").
- **Source:** Rule of Saint Benedict, ch. 16
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** benedict, monasticism, hours, psalms

### Sleeping clothed, without knives
- **Fact:** Benedict's monks sleep clothed and girded with belts or cords, but without their knives, lest they injure themselves in sleep; a lamp burns in the dormitory until morning.
- **Source:** Rule of Saint Benedict, ch. 22
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** benedict, sleep, monasticism, dormitory

### Silence after Compline
- **Fact:** After Compline, Benedict's Rule forbids anyone to speak.
- **Source:** Rule of Saint Benedict, ch. 42
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** benedict, silence, night, monasticism

### Idleness is the enemy of the soul
- **Fact:** Benedict's Rule says idleness is the enemy of the soul (*otiositas inimica est animae*). On Sundays monks are to read, and one too negligent or lazy to read is given work.
- **Source:** Rule of Saint Benedict, ch. 48
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** benedict, idleness, sunday, reading

### Nothing before the Work of God (unused)
- **Fact:** Benedict's Rule says nothing is to be preferred to the Work of God (*opus Dei*), the sung office.
- **Source:** Rule of Saint Benedict, ch. 43
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** benedict, liturgy, monasticism, priority

### Han dynasty xiumu (unused)
- **Fact:** Han dynasty officials received a day off every five days, called *xiumu* ("rest and hair-washing"); under the Tang the interval became ten days.
- **Source:** Han and Tang administrative practice as described in standard histories of Chinese officialdom; specific primary citation not checked
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** china, han, officials, calendar, bathing

### The French décade (unused)
- **Fact:** The French Republican calendar (in use 1793-1805) replaced the seven-day week with a ten-day *décade*, with *décadi*, the tenth day, as the day of rest.
- **Source:** French Republican calendar decree of 1793; abolished by Napoleon effective 1 January 1806
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** france, revolution, calendar, secularism

### The Soviet continuous week
- **Fact:** From 1 October 1929 the Soviet Union introduced the *nepreryvka*, a continuous five-day week in which workers were divided into groups (marked by colors), each with a different day off, so that production never stopped.
- **Source:** Soviet calendar reforms of 1929; see histories of the Soviet calendar (e.g. Eviatar Zerubavel, *The Seven Day Circle*, 1985)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** soviet, calendar, labor, industrialization

### Families out of sync
- **Fact:** Under the continuous week, spouses and family members assigned to different groups often had different days off, a frequent complaint.
- **Source:** Zerubavel, *The Seven Day Circle* (1985), and contemporary Soviet accounts
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** soviet, family, calendar, complaints

### The six-day week and the return of seven
- **Fact:** In 1931 the continuous week was largely replaced by a six-day week with common rest days on the 6th, 12th, 18th, 24th and 30th of the month; the seven-day week was restored in June 1940.
- **Source:** Soviet decrees of 1931 and 26 June 1940
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** soviet, calendar, week, reform

### Yuri Larin (unused)
- **Fact:** The economist Yuri Larin was a leading promoter of the continuous production week.
- **Source:** Histories of the Soviet calendar reform
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** soviet, economics, labor, reform

### Vishnu on Shesha
- **Fact:** In Hindu mythology Vishnu lies in yogic sleep (*yoga nidra*) on the serpent Shesha, also called Ananta ("endless"), on the cosmic ocean between cycles of creation; Brahma emerges on a lotus from his navel to create the world.
- **Source:** Puranic literature, e.g. *Vishnu Purana*, *Bhagavata Purana*
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** hinduism, vishnu, sleep, cosmology, serpent

### Chaturmas
- **Fact:** Chaturmas is the four-month period from Devshayani (Ashadhi) Ekadashi, the 11th day of the bright half of Ashadha, to Prabodhini Ekadashi in Kartika, during which Vishnu is said to sleep; weddings and other auspicious ceremonies are traditionally avoided.
- **Source:** Hindu calendrical and devotional tradition; *Padma Purana* and other texts
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** hinduism, chaturmas, monsoon, vishnu

### Jain monks stay put
- **Fact:** During Chaturmas Jain monks and nuns stop wandering and stay in one place, to avoid harming the many small creatures that appear during the rains.
- **Source:** Jain monastic practice (*varshavas*)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** jainism, monasticism, monsoon, ahimsa

### Buddhist rains retreat (unused)
- **Fact:** Theravada Buddhist monks observe Vassa, a three-month rains retreat during which they remain in one residence.
- **Source:** Vinaya, Mahavagga (Vassupanayika-khandhaka)
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** buddhism, vassa, monsoon, retreat

### Tulsi Vivah
- **Fact:** Around Prabodhini Ekadashi, when Vishnu wakes, many Hindu households celebrate Tulsi Vivah, the ritual marriage of the tulsi (holy basil) plant to Vishnu (or Krishna/Shaligram), which reopens the wedding season.
- **Source:** Hindu devotional practice
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** hinduism, tulsi, marriage, vishnu

### Vishnu as Bali's gatekeeper (unused)
- **Fact:** In some tellings Vishnu spends Chaturmas in the underworld as gatekeeper to the demon king Bali, honoring a promise made after his Vamana avatar.
- **Source:** Regional and Puranic tradition; precise textual source not checked
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** hinduism, bali, vamana, underworld

### Nyepi
- **Fact:** Nyepi, the Balinese Hindu new year of the Saka calendar, falls on the day after the dark moon of the ninth month and is observed as a day of silence under four prohibitions (*catur brata penyepian*): no fire or light, no work, no travel, no entertainment.
- **Source:** Balinese Hindu practice; Indonesian national holiday
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** bali, nyepi, silence, new-year, hinduism

### Ogoh-ogoh
- **Fact:** On the eve of Nyepi villages parade large demon effigies called *ogoh-ogoh* and then usually burn them; the following silence is said to make demons think the island is deserted.
- **Source:** Balinese practice (ogoh-ogoh parades became widespread from the 1980s); explanatory belief is popular tradition
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** bali, nyepi, demons, effigies

### Bali's airport closes
- **Fact:** Ngurah Rai International Airport in Bali closes for 24 hours on Nyepi.
- **Source:** Annual Indonesian aviation notices; widely reported
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** bali, nyepi, airports, travel

### The internet goes off
- **Fact:** Since 2018, mobile data service on Bali has been switched off for Nyepi at the request of Balinese religious and provincial authorities.
- **Source:** News reports, e.g. Reuters and Jakarta Post, March 2018 onward
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** bali, nyepi, internet, silence

### Pecalang
- **Fact:** Traditional village security wardens (*pecalang*) patrol the streets during Nyepi to enforce the prohibitions.
- **Source:** Balinese practice; widely reported
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** bali, nyepi, enforcement, villages

### Rest for the damned (unused)
- **Fact:** In the apocryphal Apocalypse of Paul, Paul and the archangel Michael obtain for the souls in hell a respite every Sunday; Jewish tradition similarly holds that the wicked in Gehinnom rest on the Sabbath.
- **Source:** Apocalypse of Paul (Visio Pauli), ch. 44; for the Jewish tradition, Genesis Rabbah 11:5 and the Zohar
- **Status:** legend
- **Tags:** hell, apocrypha, sunday, sabbath, afterlife

### A sixtieth of the world to come (unused)
- **Fact:** The Talmud says the Sabbath is one-sixtieth of the world to come.
- **Source:** Babylonian Talmud, Berakhot 57b
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** talmud, sabbath, afterlife, proportions

### Two Sabbaths and redemption (unused)
- **Fact:** The Talmud says that if Israel kept two Sabbaths properly, they would be redeemed immediately.
- **Source:** Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 118b
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** talmud, sabbath, redemption, messianism

### The Sabbath kept Israel (unused)
- **Fact:** The essayist Ahad Ha'am wrote that more than the Jews have kept the Sabbath, the Sabbath has kept the Jews.
- **Source:** Ahad Ha'am, essay in *Ha-Shiloah* (1898), on the Sabbath and Zionism
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** ahad-haam, zionism, sabbath, identity

### Fighting on the Sabbath (unused)
- **Fact:** First Maccabees reports that about a thousand Jews were killed when they refused to fight on the Sabbath, after which Mattathias and his followers resolved to defend themselves on that day.
- **Source:** 1 Maccabees 2:29-41
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** maccabees, war, sabbath, law

### Pompey's Sabbath siegeworks (unused)
- **Fact:** Josephus says Pompey, besieging Jerusalem in 63 BCE, had his men build siegeworks on Sabbaths, when the defenders would only resist direct attack.
- **Source:** Josephus, *Antiquities* 14.4.2-3
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** pompey, rome, jerusalem, siege, sabbath

### Shabbat elevators (unused)
- **Fact:** Some buildings with observant residents run "Shabbat elevators" that stop automatically at every floor, so no one needs to press a button.
- **Source:** Common practice in Israel and Jewish neighborhoods; Israeli law (2001) requires one in some new buildings
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** sabbath, technology, elevators, israel

### Ethiopian two Sabbaths (unused)
- **Fact:** The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church traditionally honors both Saturday and Sunday as Sabbaths.
- **Source:** Ethiopian Orthodox tradition; *Ser'ata Sanbat* and the 15th-century reforms of Emperor Zara Yaqob
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** ethiopia, christianity, sabbath, sunday

### Friday prayer, then disperse (unused)
- **Fact:** The Qur'an tells believers to leave trade at the call to Friday prayer, and after the prayer to disperse in the land and seek God's bounty, so Friday is not a day of mandated rest.
- **Source:** Qur'an 62:9-10
- **Status:** fact
- **Tags:** islam, friday, prayer, trade
